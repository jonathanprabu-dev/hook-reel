01 · GRIPLOCK, "The Edge"

A phone buzzes toward the edge of a table. Everyone has lived that half-second, so the body reacts before the brain decides to scroll. I freeze the frame mid-fall and say what the viewer is feeling, which buys two more seconds of attention.

How the agent makes them

Each ad starts as a short brief. The pipeline turns it into a finished MP4, with its own synthesized music bed matched to the mood: trap, pluck pop, comedy swing, lo-fi, tech arpeggio or bedroom pop. Changing a hook or a format means editing the brief and running it again.

Brief

Goal, audience trait (Practical, Action, Social or Emotional), platform, hook type, palette and type, in one brief.json.

Script and beats

Claude writes the beat sheet: the 0–3 s hook, a payoff every 2–3 s, one CTA. I edit for voice and truth.

Scene code

Claude Code writes the scene as HTML and CSS motion, where every frame is a function of time, so any frame can be re-rendered exactly.

Render

Headless Chromium captures 30 frames a second at 1080 × 1920 or 1080 × 1350 and pipes them to ffmpeg.

Sound design

A cue sheet in the brief drives synthesized hits, risers, whooshes and a beat, timed to the frame.

Encode

H.264 and AAC per platform, captions burned in for sound-off viewing, plus a poster frame.

Variants

Swap the hook, the first line or the colour in the brief and render A/B versions in minutes.

Learn

Judge on 3-second hold, completion and cost per result, then feed the winning hooks into the next brief.
